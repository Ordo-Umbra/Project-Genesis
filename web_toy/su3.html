Why three components?

The scalar toy can only make layered domains: a region can border its neighbouring wells but three phases never meet, so there are no Y-junctions. The URP gauge derivation's answer (§4.3.3) is a sector-membership field Ψ = (R, G, B) — three components that compete, with one dominating deep inside each domain. Now all three colours are mutually adjacent, and where they meet you get the 120° triple junctions the theory ties to colour SU(3) (the domain walls play the role of gluon-like boundary modes). The dynamics are vector Allen–Cahn — the multi-phase generalisation of the URP field update:
∂ₜη_a = D·∇²η_a − [ η_a³ − η_a + 2γ·η_a·(Σ_b η_b² − η_a²) ]
The free energy is S₃-symmetric — relabelling R→G→B is a symmetry — the discrete remnant of the global symmetry that survives deep inside sectors. Watch the domains coarsen: small grains shrink, junctions migrate, and the triple-junction count falls, exactly as in soap-foam and grain-growth physics.
