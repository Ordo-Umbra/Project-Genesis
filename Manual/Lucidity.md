# Lucidity

*A practice for coming to understanding, and for staying able to grow it.*

*Working draft: an outline, plus the first section in rough form. It has no
author on purpose. It asks to be trusted only as far as you can check it
yourself.*

---

## How to read this

This is not a set of answers. It is a set of practices, and a list of the ways
practices go wrong. Every part ends with something to do. If a part only holds
because this document says so, drop that part.

The paths are infinite. A manual can't walk them for you. All it can do is
mark where others have fallen.

---

## Outline

0. **Traps.** First, because you are already in some of them.
1. **Distinction.** Begin with the simplest difference you can actually hold.
2. **The grey.** Look at the boundaries; that is where new things find room.
3. **Persistence.** Ask what lasts, and test it rather than assume it.
4. **Many paths.** One path shows you a road. Many paths show you the land.
5. **Perspective.** Every "best" is best for a given space; name the space.
6. **Failure.** Record failures as specific facts. When the same failure
   repeats, the fault is deeper than any one instance.
7. **Pace.** Understanding has to be absorbed, not only produced.
8. **The climb.** There is no final rung. Each understanding you state becomes
   the floor for the next.
9. **Disagreeing with this manual.** How to find where it is wrong.
10. **Record of changes.** What was revised, and why.

## 0. Traps

A trap is not a wrong turn you can see. It is a path that feels like progress
from the inside. That is what makes it a trap, and it is why you rarely find
one by looking harder down the same path. You find it by stepping onto another
path and looking back.

Every trap below has the same four parts:

- **From inside:** what it feels like while you're in it.
- **The sign:** what eventually gives it away.
- **The way out:** what to do.
- **Seen in practice:** a real case, where one exists.

### The Inherited Constant

**From inside:** everything works. The rule you are using has always worked.

**The sign:** a result that should change with the situation doesn't, or one
that shouldn't change suddenly does.

**The way out:** for every rule you rely on, ask where it was first chosen,
and whether you are still standing there.

**Seen in practice:** a threshold chosen for flat, two-dimensional shapes was
carried into three and four dimensions, where it measured the wrong thing. It
happened three times in one project, with three different rules.

### The Instrument's Answer

**From inside:** you looked carefully and found nothing.

**The sign:** the way you were looking could never have shown anything else.

**The way out:** before trusting a "no", ask whether your instrument was able
to say "yes".

**Seen in practice:** a measure built for the plane silently assumed the
plane's answer. The result it was used to look for stayed invisible until the
measure itself was questioned.

### The Single Road

**From inside:** the path's assumptions look like the shape of the world.

**The sign:** you can't say what would look different if you were wrong.

**The way out:** walk a second path on purpose, preferably one you expect to
dislike.

### The Echo

**From inside:** everyone agrees, and every route arrives at the same place.

**The sign:** the routes all started from the same ground.

**The way out:** count agreement only between paths that differ in what they
take for granted. Agreement among copies is one voice, repeated.

**Seen in practice:** many runs agreed with each other for months. They all
shared one starting assumption, and about forty percent of what they
"measured" was an artefact of it.

### The Still Corner

**From inside:** it has held for a long time, so it is stable.

**The sign:** it held without ever being tested.

**The way out:** tell apart what *is* stable from what merely hasn't been
disturbed yet. Push on it gently and see whether it returns.

**Seen in practice:** a structure stood perfectly still for six hundred steps,
then came apart into a different shape that did last.

### The Forced Zero

**From inside:** a striking result. Nothing else even comes close.

**The sign:** nothing else *could* have come close, because the rules ruled it
out before you looked.

**The way out:** separate what you found from what you were bound to find.
Both are worth knowing; only one is a discovery.

### The Absorbed Failure

**From inside:** every setback is part of growth.

**The sign:** no failure has ever changed anything.

**The way out:** a failure counts as growth only when it leaves something
changed: a rule, a claim, a habit. Otherwise it is just a failure, given a
kinder name.

### The Idol

**From inside:** reverence for whoever found the thing, or for the thing
itself.

**The sign:** questions start to feel like disloyalty.

**The way out:** keep the thing open to correction, and keep no one at its
centre, including whoever wrote it down.

### The Mirror That Blames

**From inside:** "the only obstacle is myself" feels like strength.

**The sign:** it starts to explain other people's hardship as their own fault.

**The way out:** say it as *the obstacle I can always work on is myself.*
Circumstances are real. People begin in different places, with different means.

### The Flood

**From inside:** it is all arriving so fast. This must be momentum.

**The sign:** you can no longer say what the last thing meant.

**The way out:** stop. Write down what changed and what it means. Take one
step at a time, and let each one settle.

### The Final Rung

**From inside:** this is it. The picture is complete.

**The sign:** nothing new can surprise you any more.

**The way out:** treat each understanding as a floor, not a ceiling. If you
can't imagine how you might be wrong, you have stopped climbing.

### Your traps

The traps above come from one line of work. Yours will include others that
only your own context can show you. When you find one, give it the same four
parts and add it here.
